# Glossary

<p class="lede">One glossary for every Eclectic Technologies site. The course, the CIKR examples and the SMR licensing site point here rather than defining these terms again.</p>

Each entry is a plain-language reading. Where an entry names an instrument, the instrument governs, as issued in *FD — Foundational Definitions, November 2026 Edition (10.5281/zenodo.23401272)*. Terms used on only one site stay in that site’s own glossary.

<dl class="terms">
<dt id="bright-line">Bright Line</dt>
<dd>The governance boundary, drawn on the consequence of each individual action. An action is above the line when its incorrect performance could produce a consequence at or above the severity threshold set for the facility, and below it when it could not. It is not a property of a component, a boundary between architectural layers, or a classification of technologies. Written as two words, no hyphen. See <a href="#/fd-bl"><em>The Bright Line</em></a>.</dd>
<dt id="broker">Broker</dt>
<dd>A role: the qualified human who discharges the authorization obligation a Bright Line places on above-line actions. It is not an office, a job title, a software component, or a procedure telling someone to confirm. Each Broker is paired to one Line and its competence does not transfer between Lines: the <strong>Command Broker</strong> to the Bright Line, and the <strong>Influence Broker</strong> to the surveillance Bright Line, which Project 1 defines. The Broker exists to supply judgment where proof is unavailable. See <a href="#/fd-br"><em>The Broker</em></a>.</dd>
<dt id="command-broker">Command Broker</dt>
<dd>The Broker paired to the Bright Line, for industrial and control settings. The control system is architecturally incapable of executing an above-line action from an in-scope component without the Command Broker’s authorization; enforcement is structural, not procedural. Where <strong>Mode of discharge</strong> permits, the Command Broker discharges in advance, by accepting a pre-authorized envelope and its proof, and a verified gatekeeper then admits each action in the class: one role in two modes. See <a href="#/fd-br"><em>The Broker</em></a>.</dd>
<dt id="denial">Denial</dt>
<dd>The fourth of the four characteristics. The adversary blocks the repair and outside help the response depends on: access to the site, resupply, outside emergency response and communications. See <a href="#/fd-dbl"><em>Design Basis Layers</em></a>.</dd>
<dt id="design-basis">Design basis</dt>
<dd>A written, bounded, consequence-anchored commitment, made in advance and on the record, to what a facility or system is engineered to withstand and what counts as success when it is tested. It names the consequences that must not happen and the bounded set of conditions the system is built to survive, and it is the yardstick every adequacy argument is measured against. Because it is written down beforehand, anyone can check it, and everyone can tell when it has been exceeded. Borrowed from nuclear safety engineering. See <a href="#/fd-dbl"><em>Design Basis Layers</em></a>.</dd>
<dt id="design-basis-layers">Design basis layers</dt>
<dd>The three layers of design basis analysis. The <strong>design basis layer</strong> establishes the function the facility must perform. The <strong>accident layer</strong> adds disruptive events drawn from experience, under three assumptions: independence, bounded progression, and available support. The <strong>adversary layer</strong> (the military-action layer) adds an actor who sets out to stop the function, and is reached only when the actor shows all four characteristics. Each layer has five parts: the function, the conditions analyzed, the assumptions behind them, the success criterion, and the operating limits. See <a href="#/fd-dbl"><em>Design Basis Layers</em></a>.</dd>
<dt id="determinism-boundary">Determinism boundary</dt>
<dd>The scope gate that decides which components are analyzed under the Bright Line at all. A component that is deterministic within a stated input bound and formally testable as deployed is outside Bright Line analysis. Every other component is within it, including one whose determinism has never been established over a stated bound or never formally tested. Determinism sets scope only; it does not place an action above or below the line. See <a href="#/fd-ld"><em>Layer Decomposition and Determinism</em></a>.</dd>
<dt id="diverse">Diverse</dt>
<dd>Performing the same function by means that differ in principle, technology, supplier or dependency, so that no single cause can defeat them all. Diversity is the defense against common-cause failure. Examples: a digital controller backed by a hard-wired interlock; chlorination with an independent backup feed; fiber backed by licensed radio.</dd>
<dt id="envelope">Envelope</dt>
<dd>A pre-authorized region of action: an object of authorization, not of monitoring. A region is an envelope when an above-line action inside it has been authorized in advance by a qualified human on an accepted proof, and a change to the region is itself a governed act. Distinct from the <em>adversary envelope</em> of the design basis method, which is the bounded threat set. See also <strong>Operating band</strong>. See <a href="#/fd-ev"><em>The Envelope</em></a>.</dd>
<dt id="facility-class">Facility class</dt>
<dd>A category of facilities alike enough in consequence and adversary profile that a single design basis can serve the whole category, such as a water treatment plant or a 911 dispatch center. The sector framework is taken down to that kind of facility. Justified by the strategic-significance boundary.</dd>
<dt id="foundational-definitions">Foundational Definitions</dt>
<dd>The governing layer the whole body of work conforms to, held as one dated edition, currently the November 2026 Edition. Each idea is defined once, and every other document cites it by name and section rather than restating it; where wording differs, the definition governs. The eight instruments are The Bright Line, Layer Decomposition and Determinism, The Envelope, The Broker, Local Sovereignty, Mode of Discharge, Indication Integrity, and Design Basis Layers.</dd>
<dt id="four-characteristics">The four characteristics</dt>
<dd>Intentionality, persistence, precision and denial: what distinguishes a deliberate adversary from an accident. Each removes one of the accident layer’s assumptions. An actor at the military-action layer shows all four. When a real event is read back, it counts at the military-action tier only if all four are established on the record; a characteristic that cannot be shown counts as absent. Also called the four marks. See <a href="#/fd-dbl"><em>Design Basis Layers</em></a>.</dd>
<dt id="gate">Gate</dt>
<dd>The enforcement interlock: the architecture incapable of executing an above-line action without authorization. With the Line, which places the obligation, and the Broker, who authorizes, it completes a triad. The Gate never authorizes and never stands in for the Broker. See <a href="#/fd-br"><em>The Broker</em></a>.</dd>
<dt id="independent">Independent</dt>
<dd>Free of shared dependencies (power, communications, software, location, staff or vendor), so that the failure of one means cannot cause the failure of another. Without independence, diversity exists on paper but not in practice.</dd>
<dt id="indication-integrity">Indication integrity</dt>
<dd>The determination that an authorization is only as good as the picture it is granted against. Output of an in-scope component that can serve as the basis for an above-line authorization must be traceable to independently verifiable values and must not be the sole basis for the authorization. An operator acting on a corrupted or drifted picture is not blind; he is confident and wrong. See <a href="#/fd-bl-d2"><em>Indication Integrity</em></a>.</dd>
<dt id="intentionality">Intentionality</dt>
<dd>The first of the four characteristics. The adversary chooses what happens, when and in what order, watches the response and adapts to defeat it. A storm does not. See <a href="#/fd-dbl"><em>Design Basis Layers</em></a>.</dd>
<dt id="local-sovereignty">Local sovereignty</dt>
<dd>A property of a protective function, not of a device, layer, architecture or vendor. A function is locally sovereign when it executes on physical law, is colocated with the asset it protects, and needs no authority from any reachable system to act; ahead of acting, it can be altered only through a governed act. It describes what still protects the asset when the governed layer fails. It does not decide where the Bright Line falls. The construct is Project 5’s, held with the Foundational Definitions. See <a href="#/fd-ls"><em>Local Sovereignty</em></a>.</dd>
<dt id="mode-of-discharge">Mode of discharge</dt>
<dd>The determination of when the Command Broker may discharge its authorization in advance rather than in the moment. An authorization has two parts: admissibility (is the action inside a pre-authorized, verified region?) and selection (which action, at what magnitude, in what order, at what rate?). Advance discharge is permitted for an action in the eligible class if and only if the construction meets three conditions together. The human authorization is not skipped; it is relocated in time. See <a href="#/fd-bl-d1"><em>Mode of Discharge</em></a>.</dd>
<dt id="operating-band">Operating band</dt>
<dd>The region of normal operation, whose violation is a runtime event to detect and report. Its boundary is an operating limit, the approach to it is encroachment, and a crossing is an operating-limit excursion. The name keeps the everyday engineering sense of “envelope” apart from the authorization sense. See <a href="#/fd-ev"><em>The Envelope</em></a>.</dd>
<dt id="operating-limits">Operating limits</dt>
<dd>The limits within which a facility is run so that it stays inside its design basis, with the responses and reports that keep it there. The fifth part of every design basis layer. See <a href="#/fd-dbl"><em>Design Basis Layers</em></a>.</dd>
<dt id="persistence">Persistence</dt>
<dd>The second of the four characteristics. The adversary keeps the pressure on, or repeats it, so the event does not end on its own. The facility must keep operating safely for as long as it lasts. See <a href="#/fd-dbl"><em>Design Basis Layers</em></a>.</dd>
<dt id="precision">Precision</dt>
<dd>The third of the four characteristics. The adversary aims at the parts whose loss is hardest to recover from, so failures that would otherwise be chance happen together by design. See <a href="#/fd-dbl"><em>Design Basis Layers</em></a>.</dd>
<dt id="redundant">Redundant</dt>
<dd>Having more than one means of performing a function. Redundancy protects against random, independent failures but not against a common cause: identical channels share the same weaknesses, including the same cyber exploit. Redundancy is not robustness unless the redundant means are also <strong>Diverse</strong> and <strong>Independent</strong>.</dd>
<dt id="resilient">Resilient</dt>
<dd>Able to hold a defined minimum level of a critical function while degraded, and to restore full function within a defined time. Resilience is measured over time: how far function drops, for how long, and how fast it recovers. It governs events beyond the design basis, or events the design basis did not anticipate, and it is the engineering property behind deterrence by resilience. Contrast <strong>Robust</strong>.</dd>
<dt id="robust">Robust</dt>
<dd>Able to keep performing a critical function, without degradation, under the hazards and loads in its design basis. Robustness is measured as margin. It is a design basis property: past the design basis, a robust system may fail abruptly. It is the engineering property that deterrence by denial rests on. Contrast <strong>Resilient</strong>.</dd>
<dt id="robust-and-diverse">Robust and Diverse</dt>
<dd>A design principle from nuclear safety that applies across all critical infrastructure sectors. Each critical function is protected by means strong enough to withstand its design basis, and different and independent enough that no single physical, cyber or supply-chain cause defeats them all. It is the design basis foundation that resilience builds on: Robust and Diverse limits how often and how badly a function degrades; resilience governs what happens when it degrades anyway.</dd>
</dl>
