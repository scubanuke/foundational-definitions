# How to Read the Notation

<p class="lede">Every formal statement on this site is given four ways. You can skip the last two and lose nothing but the means of checking.</p>

**Plain English** comes first and carries the meaning.

**Word notation** comes second, set off in a box. It uses a few capitalized words where logic would use symbols, so that a reader who last studied logic decades ago can follow it without a key.

**Read aloud** comes third. It turns the word notation back into an ordinary sentence.

**In symbols** comes last, in standard notation, for readers who work in mathematics and want to check the statement on familiar ground.

## The capitalized words

<tr><th>Word</th><th>What it means</th><th>Symbol</th></tr>

<tr><td><b>IF … THEN</b></td><td>A one-way condition: whenever the first part holds, the second does.</td><td>$\rightarrow$</td></tr>

<tr><td><b>NOT</b></td><td>The part does not hold.</td><td>$\neg$</td></tr>

A few further symbols appear on the symbol lines. $\in$ means *is a member of*. $\subseteq$ means *is contained in*. $\geq$ means *is at least*. $\vdash$ means *proves*. The letter $a$ stands for any single action, $c$ for a component, $L$ for a Bright Line, and $\varphi$ for a protective function.

## What "Posted for Comment" means

<div class="flagbar"><span class="flag">Posted for Comment</span> &nbsp;Every page that carries a symbol line wears this label.</div>

The definitions themselves are issued instruments, published on Zenodo and citable. The formal statements are not. They come from my working paper *The Mathematics Under the Foundational Definitions* (v0.2, October 2026). That paper reads each definition against the established mathematics it corresponds to: threshold functions, invariants, forward-invariant sets, noninterference, Galois connections and games. A formal statement is a reading of a definition. It is not the definition, and where the two differ the definition governs.

The statements stay posted for comment until a mathematician has checked them. Comments are welcome. Statements marked <span class="new">NEW</span> are drafts written for this site to cover changes in the November 2026 Edition that came after the working paper. They have had no review at all.

## Three cautions

**A correspondence is not a proof.** Saying that a requirement is a forward-invariance condition tells an engineer what kind of proof the requirement demands. It does not supply the proof.

**A formula has nowhere to hide a premise.** That is the point of writing the definitions this way. Several of the corpus's assumptions surfaced only when a definition was written as a formula: one ordered scale of harm, the fidelity of the plant model, the decidability of proof. They are recorded as candidate axioms and are not settled here.

**Words govern.** Each page quotes the governing text of its instrument as issued in the November 2026 Edition. Cite the instrument, not this site.
